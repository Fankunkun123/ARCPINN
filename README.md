# ARCPINN
Enhanced Adaptive Residual Classi cation and Active Learning Strategy for PINNs in Solving Complex PDEs
## Abstract
Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) often suffer from convergence difficulties arising from the high dimensionality, non-convexity, and severe imbalance of their loss landscapes. A key limitation lies in the construction of loss functions based on the global average of residuals. This equal-weighted strategy obscures local physical features and induces gradient dilution, which excessively reduces the optimization step size in critical regions and frequently leads to convergence dilemmas. To overcome these challenges, we propose an active learning strategy based on residual classification. We use a Gaussian mixture model (GMM) to classify sampling points by residual magnitude and reconstruct the loss function accordingly. This approach directs optimization toward local features. It alleviates gradient dilution and enables targeted learning. An adaptive weighting scheme balances loss components, which mitigates imbalance. This ensures uniform optimization of physical features and improves convergence. Finally, a multi-stage optimizer leverages the complementary strengths of different optimization algorithms. This enhances accuracy and maintains training efficiency. Numerical experiments on five challenging benchmark problems demonstrate that the proposed three-level collaborative mechanism - residual classification, weight adaptation, and optimization tuning - achieves precise optimization of PDE residuals across diverse physical regions. The results show substantial improvements in both the convergence efficiency of PINN training and the accuracy of predicted solutions, providing a robust and effective framework for advancing PINN methodologies.
